# Accesso Services: Critical Infrastructure Operations

Before moving into cybersecurity consulting, I spent **4 years and 8 months** as a building engineer supporting operations at a major Class A commercial office building in Philadelphia.

This experience taught me responsibility, accountability, operational thinking, contractor coordination, tenant communication, and infrastructure reliability under real-world conditions.

While this was not a cybersecurity role, it became one of the strongest foundations for how I think about operational risk, critical infrastructure, OT/ICS security, and ServiceNow workflow ownership today.

<div class="sr-callout">

<strong>Key lesson:</strong> Infrastructure only looks simple when everything is working. Behind the scenes, reliability depends on disciplined maintenance, communication, documentation, coordination, and ownership.

</div>

## What I Was Responsible For

I served as the primary daytime engineer responsible for daily operational support, maintenance activities, tenant issues, contractor coordination, equipment checks, and building continuity.

My responsibilities included:

* responding to tenant service requests
* troubleshooting operational issues
* coordinating contractor access
* supporting building modernization projects
* maintaining preventive maintenance schedules
* documenting work and operational readings
* supporting emergency response activities
* maintaining critical building systems
* ensuring continuity of daily building operations

I regularly worked independently and covered the building alone during Saturday operations and certain weekday periods.

## Working Through COVID

One of the most significant parts of this experience was working through the COVID period as an essential employee.

While many professionals transitioned to remote work, building operations continued.

Infrastructure still had to function.

Mechanical systems still required maintenance.

Contractors still required access.

Tenants still needed support.

The experience reinforced the reality that many operational roles cannot simply pause during disruptions.

That perspective continues to influence how I think about resilience, operational continuity, and critical infrastructure.

---

## Tenant and Stakeholder Communication

A major portion of the role involved interacting with people.

The building supported a wide variety of tenants including legal organizations, financial firms, educational operations, and other commercial occupants.

Technical work alone was not enough.

Success required:

* communication
* expectation management
* professionalism
* documentation
* follow-up
* coordination

This experience helped develop the communication skills that later became valuable in cybersecurity consulting and client-facing delivery.

---

## Contractor Coordination and Infrastructure Projects

The role involved supporting contractors and vendors during maintenance, upgrades, and modernization efforts.

Examples included:

* infrastructure access support
* building modernization efforts
* equipment replacement projects
* contractor coordination
* operational scheduling
* infrastructure change activities

One notable project involved supporting building modernization efforts related to 5G infrastructure deployment.

The value of this experience was not technical telecommunications expertise.

The value was learning how infrastructure changes impact operations, access requirements, stakeholders, schedules, and ongoing business activity.

---

## What This Experience Taught Me

This role taught me lessons that continue to apply directly to cybersecurity consulting.

### Ownership Matters

Problems do not solve themselves.

Someone must own them, track them, communicate them, and ensure they are resolved.

### Communication Matters

Technical knowledge has limited value if it cannot be communicated clearly.

### Documentation Matters

Work that is not documented becomes difficult to track, validate, or improve.

### Reliability Matters

Infrastructure succeeds because people consistently perform routine work correctly.

### Operations Matter

The real challenge is often not identifying a problem.

The challenge is coordinating people, resources, timelines, and actions to solve it.

## How This Connects to Cybersecurity

The connection between building operations and cybersecurity may not be obvious at first.

However, many of the same concepts apply:

| Building Operations | Cybersecurity Operations |
| ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Work Orders         | Security Findings        |
| Maintenance Tasks   | Remediation Tasks        |
| Equipment Owners    | Assignment Groups        |
| Escalations         | Incident Response        |
| Operational Logs    | Security Documentation   |
| Verification        | Validation               |
| Completion          | Closure                  |

This operational mindset is one reason ServiceNow SecOps made sense to me.

Both environments depend on ownership, visibility, coordination, accountability, and execution.

---

## Why This Experience Matters Today

When people review my cybersecurity background, they often focus on the technical education, ServiceNow work, certifications, and security projects.

Those are important.

However, this building engineering experience may have been equally important.

It taught me how organizations actually operate.

It taught me responsibility under real constraints.

It taught me how to work with stakeholders, contractors, tenants, vendors, and technical teams.

Most importantly, it taught me that successful operations depend on people, process, communication, ownership, and execution.

That lesson continues to shape how I approach cybersecurity consulting today.
