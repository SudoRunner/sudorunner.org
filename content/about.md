## My Path

My career path has not been traditional.

Before moving into cybersecurity, I spent **4 years and 8 months as a building engineer** with Accesso Services, supporting operations at a major Class A commercial office building in Philadelphia.

That experience shaped how I think about operations, accountability, risk, communication, and follow-through. Building systems only become visible to most people when something fails. A good operator works behind the scenes to prevent problems, coordinate the right people, document work, and restore service when issues happen.

That mindset now carries into how I think about cybersecurity and ServiceNow SecOps.

---

## Operational Foundation

Building engineering gave me real exposure to operational responsibility.

I supported environments involving:

- HVAC systems
- pumps
- chillers
- electrical rooms
- fire and life-safety systems
- building controls
- access-controlled spaces
- tenant work orders
- contractor coordination
- preventive maintenance
- operational continuity

This background is one reason I am interested in OT/ICS security and cyber-physical risk.

In operational environments, cybersecurity recommendations cannot ignore uptime, safety, access, maintenance windows, vendor activity, change coordination, recovery, and real-world constraints.

---

## Cybersecurity Education

After building operations, I made a deliberate career shift into cybersecurity.

I completed a B.S. in **Cybersecurity Analytics & Operations** at Penn State, graduating <span class="sr-cum-laude">Cum Laude</span> with a **3.88 GPA**.

My academic work covered incident response, malware analysis, digital forensics, governance, risk, privacy, software development, cloud concepts, HCI, and OT/ICS security.

That education gave me technical range. The stronger story is how that foundation connects to professional delivery.

---

## Wipro and ServiceNow SecOps

At Wipro, I worked as a cybersecurity consultant supporting a **ServiceNow Vulnerability Response client engagement**.

That project showed me what cybersecurity consulting looks like in practice. The work was not just about a platform. It involved stakeholder coordination, requirements support, user story tracking, RAID management, delivery visibility, UAT support, training support, go-live readiness, and communication between client stakeholders, consultants, and offshore developers.

I learned that successful cybersecurity implementations are delivery achievements as much as technical achievements.

A good consultant has to understand the client environment, communicate clearly, track work, challenge weak assumptions when necessary, and make sure technical implementation supports the actual business process.

---

## Why ServiceNow

I chose ServiceNow because many organizations are good at finding vulnerabilities, but struggle to turn those findings into accountable remediation.

The harder problem is turning vulnerability data into:

- clear ownership
- remediation work
- prioritization
- validation
- exception handling
- reporting
- closure
- accountability

That is why ServiceNow SecOps and Vulnerability Response interest me.

My long-term interest is especially connected to operational environments where security work needs to respect uptime, safety, maintenance windows, asset ownership, and recovery constraints.

---

I am not positioning myself as only a developer.

Development skill matters, and I continue to build technical depth. But my strongest professional lane is consulting, delivery, and security workflow translation.
